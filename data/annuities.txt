# Annuities Overview

## What Is an Annuity?

An annuity is a financial product sold by insurance companies that provides income payments over a period of time, typically for retirement. It is funded with a lump sum or a series of contributions and is designed to provide a steady income stream.

## Types of Annuities

### Immediate vs Deferred
An immediate annuity begins paying income shortly after a single premium is paid. A deferred annuity accumulates value for years before payments begin, allowing growth during the accumulation phase.

### Fixed Annuities
A fixed annuity provides a guaranteed rate of return on contributions and a guaranteed income payout. It carries low risk because the insurer promises the rate.

### Variable Annuities
A variable annuity lets the owner invest in subaccounts resembling mutual funds. Returns and income fluctuate with investment performance, so the owner bears market risk.

### Indexed Annuities
An indexed annuity earns interest linked to a market index, such as the S&P 500, with a guaranteed minimum return. It balances the guarantees of a fixed annuity with some upside growth potential.

## Annuity Riders and Options

Annuities may include riders such as guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefits, spousal continuation, and death benefits. Buyers choose payout options including life only, life with a period certain, and joint-and-survivor for couples.

## Tax Treatment

Earnings inside an annuity grow on a tax-deferred basis. Withdrawals before age 59.5 may be subject to a 10% early withdrawal penalty, and ordinary income tax applies to the growth portion of payouts. Contributions are generally made with after-tax dollars.

## Surrender Charges

Many annuities charge a surrender fee if funds are withdrawn early, typically declining over the first several contract years. Understanding surrender periods is important before purchasing, since withdrawals during this window are costly.
