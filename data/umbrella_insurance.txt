# Umbrella Insurance Overview

## What Is Umbrella Insurance?

Personal umbrella insurance is an extra liability policy that provides coverage above the limits of your underlying homeowners, auto, and watercraft policies. It is intended for people who want more protection against large lawsuits than their primary policies offer.

## How It Works

### Primary Layer vs Excess Layer
An umbrella policy sits on top of your primary coverage. It pays only after the underlying policy limits are exhausted. Underlying policies must meet a minimum required limit, such as 250,000 general liability and 250,000 per-person bodily injury for auto.

### Broadened Coverage
Umbrella policies extend liability to risks not covered by the underlying policies, including slander, libel, false arrest, and rental unit liability. It typically protects worldwide for personal activities.

### Legal Defense Costs
Umbrella policies often provide higher defense coverage, including the cost to defend lawsuits, which can be paid above and beyond the policy limits.

## Who Should Buy It

Umbrella insurance is commonly recommended for individuals with significant assets, homeowners with pools or trampolines, parents of teenage drivers, landlords, and people who host events on their property.

## Typical Limits

Umbrella limits commonly range from 1 million to 5 million dollars of additional coverage. Premiums are relatively low for the coverage amount compared to primary policies.
