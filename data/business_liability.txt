# Business Liability Insurance

## Overview

Business liability insurance protects a company from claims that can arise from normal business operations, including bodily injury, property damage, and personal injury claims. It is essential for protecting both small and large businesses against financial loss.

## Types of Business Liability Coverage

### General Liability Insurance
General liability insurance provides coverage for bodily injury, property damage, medical payments, and personal or advertising injury. It covers claims such as a customer slipping and falling at a business location or a product causing injury.

### Professional Liability Insurance
Also known as errors and omissions (E&O) insurance, this covers claims of negligence, mistakes, or failure to perform professional duties. It is critical for service-based businesses like consultants, accountants, and lawyers.

### Product Liability Insurance
Product liability coverage protects manufacturers, distributors, and retailers against claims of injury or damage caused by products they sell or manufacture. This includes coverage for design defects, manufacturing defects, and inadequate warnings.

### Commercial Auto Insurance
This covers vehicles owned and used by a business, including liability and physical damage coverage for company cars, trucks, and vans used for business purposes.

### Cyber Liability Insurance
Cyber liability coverage helps businesses respond to data breaches and cyberattacks. It covers the costs of notifying affected customers, legal fees, regulatory fines, forensic investigations, and sometimes business interruption losses.

## Policy Structures

### Occurrence-Based Policies
Coverage is triggered by the incident itself, regardless of when the claim is filed, as long as the incident occurred during the policy period.

### Claims-Made Policies
Coverage is triggered when a claim is filed during the policy period, even if the incident occurred earlier. This requires continuous coverage to protect against retroactive claims.

## Common Exclusions

Business liability policies typically exclude intentional acts, contractual liability, employee injuries (covered by workers' compensation), and pollution or environmental damage. Businesses may need separate policies for these risks.
