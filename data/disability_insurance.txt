# Disability Insurance Overview

## What Is Disability Insurance?

Disability insurance replaces a portion of your income when you cannot work because of illness or injury. It is designed to protect earning power, and it comes in short-term and long-term forms.

## Short-Term vs Long-Term Disability

### Short-Term Disability (STD)
STD covers a portion of income for a few weeks to a few months after a qualifying condition. It typically begins after a short elimination period of 0 to 14 days and pays benefits for up to 6 months.

### Long-Term Disability (LTD)
LTD covers income loss for extended periods, often until retirement age. It usually has a longer elimination period of 90 or 180 days and pays a percentage of income, commonly 60% to 66%, up to a monthly maximum.

## Key Policy Terms

### Own-Occupation vs Any-Occupation
Own-occupation coverage pays if you cannot perform your own occupation. Any-occupation coverage pays only if you cannot work in any occupation for which you are reasonably suited. Own-occupation is more protective.

### Elimination Period
This is the waiting period between the onset of disability and when benefits begin. Longer elimination periods lower the premium.

### Benefit Period
The maximum length of time benefits are payable, ranging from a few months for STD coverage to age 65 or beyond for LTD coverage.

### Residual and Partial Benefits
Residual benefits pay a prorated amount if you return to work part time, while a definition of total disability determines when full benefits are payable.

## What Is Usually Excluded

Disability policies exclude pre-existing conditions, self-inflicted injuries, and disabilities from normal pregnancy or routine medical care. Group disability coverage is often provided by employers, while individuals can buy supplemental policies.

## Tax Considerations

Benefits are generally tax-free if the premiums were paid with after-tax dollars. If the employer paid the premium, benefits are usually taxable as income.
