# Renters Insurance Overview

## What Is Renters Insurance?

Renters insurance is a type of property and liability policy for tenants that protects personal property inside a rented apartment, house, or condominium. It does not cover the building itself, which is the landlord's responsibility.

## Coverage Components

### Personal Property Coverage
Personal property coverage reimburses you for belongings such as furniture, clothing, electronics, and kitchen items damaged or destroyed by a covered peril. This includes theft, fire, smoke, vandalism, and certain water damage. Limits apply per category, and high-value items such as jewelry, watches, and collectibles may require scheduled coverage or an endorsement.

### Loss of Use (Additional Living Expenses)
If a covered loss makes your rental unit uninhabitable, loss of use coverage pays for temporary lodging, meals, and related costs while repairs are completed.

### Personal Liability
Personal liability coverage protects you if you are legally responsible for bodily injury or property damage to others, including injuries that occur in your rental unit or caused by you away from home.

### Medical Payments
Medical payments coverage pays for medical bills of guests injured in your rental, regardless of fault, up to a specified per-person limit.

## Common Exclusions

Renters policies typically exclude flooding, earthquakes, and damage from pests or gradual wear. Separate flood or earthquake policies are required for those risks. Roommates not named on the policy are generally not covered, and each roommate needs their own policy.

## Why Landlords Require It

Many landlords require renters insurance as a condition of the lease because it shields their own policy from tenants' liability claims and reimburses the landlord's building policy for accidental tenant-caused damage.
