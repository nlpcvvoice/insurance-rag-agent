# Flood Insurance Overview

## What Is Flood Insurance?

Flood insurance is a specialized policy that covers structural damage and contents loss caused by flooding. Standard homeowners, renters, and commercial property policies do not cover flooding, so a separate flood policy must be purchased.

## Why Standard Policies Exclude Flood

Flood risk is geographically concentrated and potentially catastrophic, so private insurers exclude it from standard property policies. In the United States, the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), administered by the government, provides most residential flood coverage.

## What Flood Insurance Covers

### Building Coverage
Covers the structure, foundation, built-in appliances, and permanently installed systems such as plumbing and electrical. Some private policies also cover detached structures and pool equipment.

### Contents Coverage
Covers personal belongings such as furniture, clothing, electronics, and appliances, usually on an actual cash value basis under the NFIP.

## What Is Not Covered

Flood policies generally exclude damage to basements and items stored in basements, damage caused by moisture or mold that is not flood related, and damage from sewer backup unless flood waters directly caused it. Currency, precious metals, and valuable papers are frequently excluded.

## How Premiums Are Determined

Premiums depend on the building's flood zone, elevation relative to the base flood elevation, structure type, and coverage amounts. Homes in high-risk flood zones typically require a flood insurance purchase as a condition of a federally backed mortgage.

## The Waiting Period

There is commonly a 30-day waiting period after purchase before flood coverage becomes effective, so property owners cannot buy coverage at the last minute before an event. A few limited exceptions apply to loan-related purchases.

## NFIP vs Private Flood Insurance

The NFIP offers fixed limits and standard rates, while private flood insurers can provide higher limits, replacement cost on contents, and additional living expense coverage. Private coverage is increasingly used for high-value properties or where NFIP limits are insufficient.
