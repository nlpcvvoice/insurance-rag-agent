# Travel Insurance Overview

## What Is Travel Insurance?

Travel insurance provides financial protection for losses that occur during a trip, covering medical emergencies, trip cancellation, delays, and lost baggage. It is typically purchased for international travel, cruises, and expensive or non-refundable trips.

## Main Coverage Types

### Trip Cancellation and Interruption
Trip cancellation reimburses prepaid, non-refundable trip costs if you must cancel for a covered reason such as illness, injury, or death of the traveler or an immediate family member. Trip interruption coverage pays for unused portions and extra transportation if a trip must be cut short for a covered reason.

### Emergency Medical and Evacuation
Emergency medical coverage pays for hospital care, doctors, and prescriptions abroad, where domestic health insurance often does not apply. Medical evacuation coverage transports you to the nearest adequate facility or back home when medically necessary, which can be extremely expensive without coverage.

### Baggage and Personal Effects
Reimburses the cost to repair or replace lost, damaged, or delayed baggage and personal belongings, subject to item limits.

### Travel Delay
Pays for meals, hotels, and additional accommodation when a trip is delayed a certain number of hours due to a covered reason such as weather or mechanical failure.

## Covered Reasons vs Cancel for Any Reason

Standard policies list specific covered reasons for cancellation. A cancel-for-any-reason upgrade allows cancellation for reasons not listed, usually requiring that the policy be purchased within a short period after the first trip deposit and reimbursing a percentage of trip cost rather than the full amount.

## Exclusions and Limitations

Travel policies exclude pre-existing medical conditions unless a waiver is purchased, and they exclude risky activities such as extreme sports unless a rider is added. Alcohol-related claims and travel to certain countries under state warning may also be excluded.

## When to Buy

Travel insurance should be purchased soon after booking, before a pre-existing condition window closes, and well outside the waiting periods that apply to cancel-for-any-reason coverage. Early purchase also locks in the premium and protects against itinerary changes.
