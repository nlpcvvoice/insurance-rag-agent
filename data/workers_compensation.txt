# Workers Compensation Insurance Overview

## What Is Workers Compensation?

Workers compensation insurance provides wage replacement and medical benefits to employees who suffer work-related injuries or illnesses. In most states, it is mandatory for employers, and it protects the employer from most liability lawsuits by employees.

## Core Benefits

### Medical Benefits
Pays for medical treatment, hospital stays, prescription drugs, physical therapy, and rehabilitation associated with the work injury or illness.

### Wage Replacement
Provides a portion of lost wages, typically two-thirds of the average weekly wage, while the employee cannot work. Benefits are usually subject to weekly minimum and maximum amounts set by state law.

### Disability Categories
Temporary total, temporary partial, permanent total, and permanent partial disability are the main benefit categories, each with different benefit levels and durations.

### Death Benefits
Pays funeral expenses and a percentage of wages to the dependents of a worker who dies from a work-related cause.

## How Premiums Are Calculated

Premiums are based on the employer's industry classification, payroll, and claims history. High-risk industries such as construction, manufacturing, and logging have higher rates than office work. A poor experience modification factor (mod rate) from past claims increases premiums.

## What Is Not Covered

Typically excluded are injuries while intoxicated, self-inflicted injuries, willful misconduct, fighting at work, and injuries that occur off the job. Independent contractors are generally not covered under an employer's policy.

## Managing Claims

Prompt reporting of workplace injuries reduces claim costs. Employers must document incidents, direct employees to approved medical providers, and coordinate with the insurer to return workers to work safely.
