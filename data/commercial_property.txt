# Commercial Property Insurance Overview

## What Is Commercial Property Insurance?

Commercial property insurance protects business-owned buildings, contents, equipment, inventory, and structures from covered losses such as fire, theft, wind, and certain water damage.

## Covered Components

### Building and Contents
Covers the physical structure and its contents, including furniture, machinery, inventory, and tenant improvements. Contents can be insured at replacement cost or actual cash value, with replacement cost being the more common choice.

### Business Income (Business Interruption)
Replaces lost income and continues paying some operating expenses when a covered loss forces the business to suspend operations. It is critical for retailers, restaurants, and manufacturers whose revenue depends on ongoing operations.

### Equipment Breakdown
Covers the repair or replacement cost of mechanical, electrical, and HVAC equipment that breaks down, plus related business income losses.

### Extra Expense
Pays for the additional costs of staying in business after a loss, such as renting temporary premises or expediting delivery of needed equipment.

## Forms and Deductibles

Property policies are written on a named-peril or special form basis. Special form (all-risk) covers any cause of loss not specifically excluded, while named-peril covers only the perils listed. Deductibles apply per loss, and lower deductibles increase premiums.

## Common Exclusions

Commercial property policies exclude flooding, earthquakes, and sewer backup unless an endorsement is added, as well as damage from wear and tear, gradual deterioration, and most kinds of equipment malfunction covered under separate policies.

## Valuation

Policies that insure at replacement cost pay to replace an item with a similar new item. Actual cash value subtracts depreciation. Replacement cost valuation requires adequate limits, or a coinsurance penalty may apply at claim time.
